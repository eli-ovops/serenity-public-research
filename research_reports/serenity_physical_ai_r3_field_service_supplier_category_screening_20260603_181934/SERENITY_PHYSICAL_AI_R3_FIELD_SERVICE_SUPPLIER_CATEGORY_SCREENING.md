## 1. Executive Summary

R3 decision: field service / spare parts / uptime monitoring remains a valid Serenity-style Physical AI bottleneck lane, but only two categories are ready to proceed directly to company-level evidence review: OEM service network and spare parts logistics. These have the cleanest installed-base linkage, recurring service logic, customer urgency and lock-in potential.

Strongest categories:
- OEM service network: strongest direct installed-base linkage and best evidence of recurring support, service footprint, diagnostics, repair and parts lock-in.
- Spare parts logistics: strong when parts are proprietary, safety-critical, obsolete-model, warranty-linked or OEM-authorized.

Priority but not yet company-review-ready:
- Safety inspection / compliance service: standards-backed and real, but recurring revenue and supplier concentration must be proven.
- Calibration / testing service: physically necessary for uptime/accuracy, but needs production-impact and repeat-cycle evidence.
- Predictive maintenance and uptime monitoring software: promising, but blocked if they are dashboard-only or lack paid adoption and uptime proof.

Weak / diluted:
- Independent system integrator service and field technician / deployment service are operationally real but risk becoming labor-commodity categories unless certification, recurring contracts, proprietary access or margin evidence is proven.
- Broad automation conglomerates are benchmark-only unless service/spares/uptime exposure is material and separable.

No-go:
- Generic spare parts distributors remain no-go unless upgraded into proprietary, OEM-authorized, obsolete-model or safety-critical parts economics.

## 3. Evidence Requirements By Category

### OEM service network
- E3: Named OEM service plans, installed-base support programs, service locations, technician footprint, or repeat customer support workflows.
- E4: Filings/annual reports disclose robotics service, spare parts, field services, modernization, digital services, support revenue, or contract-backed service contribution.
- E5: Service margin/cash-flow, renewal/attach-rate proxy, repair volume, installed-base scale plus service footprint, or clear strategic service economics.
### Spare parts logistics
- E3: Named parts programs, parts warehouses, obsolete model support, repair-part counts, dispatch network, or customer support workflows.
- E4: Parts/aftersales/spares revenue, spare-parts contract revenue, maintenance parts contribution, or disclosed installed-base service category.
- E5: High fill rates, repair volumes, recurring parts demand, margin/cash-flow evidence, proprietary/obsolete parts lock-in, or inventory turning into profitable uptime economics.
### Predictive maintenance
- E3: Named predictive-maintenance service, connected robot/system monitoring, failure-prediction function used in real operations.
- E4: Paid predictive-maintenance module, premium service contract, software support revenue, or disclosed maintenance-support gross profit.
- E5: Measured downtime reduction, failure prediction accuracy, renewal rates, margin/cash-flow proof, or scaled connected installed base.
### Uptime monitoring software
- E3: Named fleet/robot monitoring service, real customer or service workflow, alerts tied to support process.
- E4: Subscription, maintenance/support revenue, paid portal, service contract bundling, or operational-system support revenue.
- E5: Paid renewals, dispatch integration, downtime reduction, customer ROI, gross margin or cash-flow contribution.
### Calibration / testing service
- E3: Named calibration/recommissioning service, production acceptance testing, metrology/vision QA used at customer sites.
- E4: Calibration, testing, metrology, QA service revenue or backlog contribution, or recurring service contracts.
- E5: Repeat calibration cycles, high-margin service revenue, scrap/downtime reduction, customer retention, and cash-flow proof.
### Safety inspection / compliance service
- E3: Standards-backed safety validation, named compliance service, inspection cadence, CE/ANSI/ISO/OSHA support in production workflows.
- E4: Safety validation/training/service revenue, compliance contract revenue, safety-service backlog, or segment disclosure.
- E5: Recurring inspection cadence, liability-driven customer urgency, margin/cash-flow proof, certification scarcity, and renewal evidence.
### Independent system integrator service
- E3: Named repeat accounts, certified integrator status, mixed-vendor support contracts, emergency response workflows.
- E4: Service/maintenance contracts, recurring support revenue, retrofit/recommissioning backlog, or project-to-service conversion evidence.
- E5: Service margins, renewal rates, customer retention, dispatch footprint, certification lock-in, and repeat-account economics.
### Field technician / deployment service
- E3: Named field support teams, technician footprint, commissioning service, emergency repair or maintenance programs.
- E4: Field-service revenue, service-contract backlog, installation-to-service conversion, warranty/service revenue disclosure.
- E5: Utilization, margin/cash flow, attach rates, response-time premium, certified labor scarcity, and renewal evidence.
### Generic spare parts distributors
- E3: Named robot-specific authorized parts program or specialized obsolete/safety-critical parts program.
- E4: Robot-specific parts revenue, authorized distributor revenue, proprietary parts contracts, or service-kit backlog.
- E5: Margins, proprietary authorization, fill-rate economics, customer urgency, safety-critical lock-in, and recurring installed-base pull.
### Broad automation conglomerates
- E3: Named robot/automation service category within a conglomerate with visible installed-base support.
- E4: Separable robotics/service/spares revenue, segment margin, or service backlog.
- E5: Service economics are separable, material, recurring, and not swamped by unrelated automation businesses.

## 4. Hard STOP / BLOCKED Gates

Gate definitions:
- **SERVICE-NO-INSTALLED-BASE**: Stop if the service category lacks a real installed robot/automation base that creates future maintenance demand.
- **SERVICE-DASHBOARD-ONLY**: Stop if monitoring or predictive maintenance is only visualization, with no paid adoption, dispatch integration or downtime reduction proof.
- **SERVICE-LABOR-COMMODITY**: Stop if the work is ordinary local labor with no certification, OEM authorization, proprietary tooling, data access or repeat contract lock-in.
- **SERVICE-GENERIC-PARTS**: Stop if parts are generic, easily substituted, non-safety-critical and not linked to warranty, uptime or obsolete-model support.
- **SERVICE-BROAD-EXPOSURE-DILUTED**: Stop if a public company is too broad for service/spares/uptime exposure to be separable or material.
- **SERVICE-NO-REVENUE-DISCLOSURE**: Stop or keep at category-only if no service, support, spare parts, maintenance, aftersales, backlog or contract revenue evidence exists.
- **SERVICE-CUSTOMER-CONCENTRATION**: Stop if evidence is dominated by one customer and cannot support broad category adoption.
- **SERVICE-NO-UPTIME-PROOF**: Stop if the category cannot show customer urgency through uptime, downtime reduction, safety, ROI or renewal evidence.
